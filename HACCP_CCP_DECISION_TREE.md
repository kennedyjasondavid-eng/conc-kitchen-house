## How to read this
This document answers, for every dish: **which control points are genuinely Critical (CCPs)** — where loss of control means unsafe food and no later step recovers it — **versus which are ordinary Control Points / prerequisite programs (CP/PRP)** that good practice handles. The CCP set follows from each dish's **process class** (P1–P4); the decision-tree table below is the reusable determination, and §4 lists which dishes fall in each class.

## 1. The Codex CCP decision tree
Applied to each significant hazard at each step:
- **Q1** — Is a control measure in place?
- **Q2** — Is this step specifically *designed* to eliminate/reduce the hazard to an acceptable level? *(Yes → CCP.)*
- **Q3** — Could contamination/growth reach unacceptable levels here?
- **Q4** — Will a *subsequent* step eliminate/reduce it? *(Yes → not a CCP; No → CCP.)*

## 3. Essential CCP set by process class
- **P1 — No-cook / cold:** **CCP-3** (cold hold) · **CCP-6** (allergen). No kill step → cold-chain + hygiene PRPs carry the load.
- **P2 — Same-day cook-serve:** **CCP-1** (cook) · **CCP-5** (hot-hold/service) · **CCP-6**. No chilled hold → no CCP-2/3/4.
- **P3 — Cook-chill-transport-reheat:** **CCP-1 · CCP-2 · CCP-3 · CCP-4 · CCP-5 · CCP-6** (the full set). A sauce its method cooks is made ahead the same way (cooked, cooled, held cold), whatever cold route it travels.
- **P4 — Dessert / baking:** **CCP-1** (bake, or cook a pudding) · **CCP-6**, plus **CCP-2** (cooling) and **CCP-3** (cold hold) when the dessert is time/temperature controlled — dairy or egg in it, a moist or filled dessert (pie, tart, pudding, custard, cream), or one cooked on the stove. Served cold or at room temperature → no CCP-4/5.
- **Always CP/PRP (every class):** receiving, storage, thawing, portioning, sanitation/hygiene, thermometer calibration.
