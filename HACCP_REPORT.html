Overview

What this is, and why it exists

CONC runs a multi-site cook-chill kitchen: cook at Bloor & Rexdale → blast-chill → vacuum-pack → refrigerate → van between sites → reheat → serve ~300 shelter residents. This is a complete, documented HACCP food-safety system for that operation — the analysis, the controls, the record design, and the in-app HACCP card that keeps the recipe-level view current.

CONC is legally required only to pass a standard Ontario (O. Reg. 493/17) health inspection. This HACCP system is voluntary — built to demonstrate, in a city-contract bid, that CONC operates a kitchen that is documented and controlled above the regulatory floor. A funder or partner can see exactly how food safety is managed, not just trust that it is.

The single framing rule that runs through every document: Ontario binds almost no numbers. Only three are mandatory — cold-hold ≤4 °C, hot-hold ≥60 °C, and ≤4 °C in transit (s. 27). Every other limit (cooking, cooling, reheating, shelf-life, ROP) is CONC's own evidence-based standard, adopted to satisfy the s. 26(2) performance duty ("food processed in a manner that makes it safe to eat") and drawn from Health Canada / FDA / UK FSA. Stating it that way — "our validated standard," not "the regulation requires" — is what makes the plan honest under audit.

The consumers are a highly susceptible population (shelter residents, many immunocompromised), so every borderline decision takes the stricter path. That single fact raises the risk tier of the whole operation and is why the controls below are conservative by design.

CCP-2 · Cool60→20 °C ≤2 h, 20→4 °C ≤4 h · blast chiller

The System, Component by Component

Eight pieces, and the reasoning behind each

The system is deliberately layered: a plain-language front sheet, a formal plan, the analytical core, the records layer, and the software that keeps it all in sync with the live menu. Each is a separate document or build so any one can be read, audited, or revised on its own.

The cited regulatory ground-truth: what Ontario / Toronto Public Health / Health Canada / FDA / FSA actually require, the binding-vs-best-practice split, and the seven architect decisions that resolved the open questions.

3 · The HACCP Plan (spine)

You cannot analyze hazards without first scoping the process exactly — where it begins (receiving), where it ends (service or documented discard), and who eats the food. Grouping 224 recipes into three process flows (the "process approach") is what makes a plan of this size tractable.

5 · Per-Dish CCP Decision-Tree

HACCP_CCP_DECISION_TREE.md
Every one of the 224 live dishes run through the decision tree → its process class and the CCPs that apply to it, generated automatically from the production feed.
224 recipes can't each carry a hand-written plan. Generating the determination from the menu data means it is deterministic, reproducible, and self-updating when the menu changes — and it surfaces the dishes that need the most attention (the 53 vacuum-packed/ROP items, the no-kill-step cold items).
✓Done: generated for all 224 (P3 81 / P2 60 / P1 83); regenerates via a script on any feed change.
→Next: classify the 23 dishes whose process is inferred (empty logistics metadata).

6 · Monitoring, Corrective Actions, Verification & Records

HACCP_MONITORING_RECORDS.md
Principles 4–7: the per-CCP master plan (what's monitored, how often, by whom; the corrective action; the verification; the record) plus a software design to capture temperatures on the existing HUB/DOOR backbone.
A control only counts if it is monitored, corrected when it fails, verified by someone, and recorded. CONC's controls already exist as cook-card instructions — the missing layer is the record. The design reuses the apps the kitchen already runs, so it's buildable without standing up a new system.
✓Done: master plan + corrective/verification programs + the temperature-capture schema designed.
→Next: build it — Phase A capture → Phase B publish records → Phase C pre-service gate.

7 · Record-Keeping Guide

HACCP_RECORDKEEPING_GUIDE.md
The audit-ready playbook: the minimal record set, the hazard→CCP→limit→control→monitor→record→fix combinations in one table, simplicity tactics, and the exact questions an auditor will ask.
A record system fails two ways — too heavy and staff stop filling it in, or too thin and it fails the audit. The target is the minimum set that proves control, captured in the workflow staff already do. 2-year retention and a 7-day retained food sample are baked in.
✓Done: forms, combinations, retention (2 yr) + food-sample rule, auditor checklist.
→Next: the digital capture (component 6) replaces the paper clipboard.

8 · In-App HACCP Card (MISE)

conc-recipe-hub
A per-dish HACCP card generated by the same deterministic engine as the Cook's Card — printable, and baked into the recipe XLSX export — covering process class, CCPs, limits, hazards, and hold/shelf-life.
Safety happens at the instruction the cook reads, not in a binder on a shelf. Generating the HACCP card right next to the Cook's Card means it is always current with the recipe, and the stored recipe files carry the CCP information with them. This is also where the fish (155→158 °F) and ground-poultry (→74 °C) cook-temp corrections landed.

The shape is the reassuring part: almost every high-impact control — cooking to temp, blast-chilling, allergen segregation, vacuum-packing — is already standard practice (the green cluster, low operational change).
